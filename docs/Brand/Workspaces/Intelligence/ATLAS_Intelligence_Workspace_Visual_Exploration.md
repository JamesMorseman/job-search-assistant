**Owner:** Sara — Visual Design
**Authority:** Project Master (Claude Ash)
**Status:** Visual Identity Exploration

---

# Core Problem

Radar succeeded because it found its object:

```text
Opportunity Signal
```

Pipeline succeeded because it found its object:

```text
Progression Flow
```

Intelligence currently has no equivalent.

This is why Intelligence still feels abstract.

The workspace has a purpose:

```text
What does Atlas understand?
```

But it lacks a visual manifestation of understanding itself.

The next decision is therefore not:

```text
What should Intelligence show?
```

It is:

```text
What does understanding look like?
```

---

# Option A — Insight Card

## Structure

Example:

```text
Insight

Structural opportunities continue increasing.

Transportation firms generated
62% of interview activity.

View Supporting Evidence
```

---

## Strengths

Simple.

Scalable.

Easy to understand.

Fits existing card architecture.

---

## Weaknesses

Feels dangerously close to:

```text
Analytics dashboard card
```

or

```text
reporting widget
```

Cards naturally encourage fragmentation.

The workspace risks becoming:

```text
Collection of observations
```

rather than:

```text
Atlas understanding
```

---

## Assessment

Useful supporting object.

Not strong enough as the primary Intelligence identity.

---

# Option B — Intelligence Brief

## Structure

Example:

```text
Atlas Brief

Observation

Transportation hiring activity continues increasing.

Interpretation

The strongest interview conversion
currently originates from bridge and
transportation-focused firms.

Recommendation

Prioritize transportation opportunities
during the next application cycle.
```

---

## Strengths

Feels thoughtful.

Feels interpreted.

Creates distinction from metrics.

Supports evidence.

Supports recommendations.

---

## Weaknesses

Can become verbose.

Can feel document-like.

May reduce scanability.

---

## Assessment

Strong candidate.

Much closer to Intelligence than cards.

---

# Option C — Strategic Narrative Object

## Structure

Example:

```text
Atlas Understanding

Observation

Bridge engineering opportunities
continue increasing.

Interpretation

Demand appears strongest among
mid-sized transportation consultancies.

Perspective

Current market conditions favor
specialization over broad civil positioning.
```

---

## Strengths

Most aligned with:

```text
Understanding
```

rather than:

```text
Reporting
```

Feels uniquely Atlas.

Creates strategic context.

Encourages reflection.

---

## Weaknesses

Less operational.

Requires stronger writing quality.

May feel abstract for some users.

---

## Assessment

Most philosophically aligned.

Potentially difficult to scale alone.

# Option D — Intelligence Lens (New Concept)

## Core Idea

Atlas does not produce cards.

Atlas produces lenses.

A lens represents:

```text
How Atlas currently sees the world.
```

Examples:

```text
Market Lens

Career Lens

Opportunity Lens

Skill Lens

Compensation Lens
```

Selecting a lens reveals:

* observations
* interpretations
* recommendations
* supporting evidence

---

## Example

```text
Opportunity Lens

Atlas currently observes:

Structural opportunities increasing.

Atlas interprets:

Demand concentration is strongest
among transportation firms.

Atlas recommends:

Prioritize bridge-focused opportunities.
```

---

## Strengths

Unique.

Ownable.

Scalable.

Consistent with:

```text
Atlas interprets.
```

because a lens literally changes perspective.

Creates strong workspace identity.

---

## Weaknesses

Requires onboarding.

Less immediately familiar.

---

## Assessment

Most distinctive concept explored.

# Recommendation

## Primary Object

### Atlas Brief

The primary Intelligence object should be:

```text
Atlas Brief
```

Reason:

It naturally combines:

```text
Observation
↓
Interpretation
↓
Recommendation
```

which mirrors the Atlas role exactly.

---

# Supporting Object

### Intelligence Lens

The workspace should organize briefs through:

```text
Lenses
```

Examples:

```text
Opportunities

Market

Skills

Compensation

Career Trajectory
```

Lenses become the navigation model.

Briefs become the content model.

# Relationship to Recommendation Cards

Recommendation Cards answer:

```text
What should I do?
```

Atlas Briefs answer:

```text
Why does Atlas think that?
```

Recommendation Cards remain action-oriented.

Briefs remain understanding-oriented.

This distinction is critical.

---

# Relationship to Ask Atlas

Atlas Briefs are:

```text
Proactive intelligence.
```

Ask Atlas is:

```text
Interactive intelligence.
```

Example:

Brief:

```text
Transportation firms currently generate
the highest interview conversion.
```

User asks:

```text
Why?
```

Ask Atlas explains.

The relationship becomes:

```text
Atlas Brief
↓
Curiosity
↓
Ask Atlas
```

---

# Final Recommendation

Intelligence should not be built around:

```text
Cards
Charts
Metrics
Reports
```

It should be built around:

```text
Atlas Briefs
```

organized through:

```text
Intelligence Lenses
```

because this is the first model that genuinely feels like:

```text
ATLAS scans.

Atlas interprets.

Ask Atlas communicates.
```

rather than:

```text
Data was collected.

Charts were generated.
```

The Intelligence Workspace should feel like receiving a strategic briefing from Atlas, not opening an analytics dashboard.
