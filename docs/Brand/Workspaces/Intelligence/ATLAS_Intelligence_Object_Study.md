**Owner:** Sara — Visual Design

# Core Observation

The Intelligence workspace is now structurally different from every other workspace.

Radar owns:

```text
Discovery
```

Pipeline owns:

```text
Progression
```

Intelligence owns:

```text
Interpretation
```

This creates a unique challenge.

Discovery naturally produces:

```text
Signals
```

Progression naturally produces:

```text
Flows
```

Interpretation does not naturally produce cards, charts, tables, or dashboards.

Interpretation produces:

```text
Understanding
```

The Intelligence object therefore cannot merely display information.

It must represent:

```text
Atlas understanding something.
```

---

# Evaluation A — Atlas Brief

## Can the Brief become the object?

Potentially.

However only if the Brief is reinterpreted.

The mistake would be treating Briefs as:

```text
Documents
```

or

```text
Cards
```

because then Intelligence immediately becomes:

```text
Report Center
```

which is exactly what we are trying to avoid.

---

## Successful Brief Model

An Atlas Brief is not a report.

An Atlas Brief is a structured interpretation.

Example:

```text
Atlas Brief

Observation

Structural opportunities continue increasing.

Interpretation

Demand is becoming concentrated among
transportation-focused consultancies.

Implication

Specialization is increasingly valuable.

Recommendation

Prioritize transportation firms.
```

---

## Why This Works

The Brief naturally mirrors:

```text
Observation
↓
Interpretation
↓
Implication
↓
Recommendation
```

which is exactly what Atlas does.

---

## Why It Is Not Merely A Card

Cards are generally:

```text
Atomic
```

Briefs are:

```text
Explanatory
```

A Recommendation Card tells users:

```text
Do this.
```

A Brief tells users:

```text
Here is why Atlas understands reality this way.
```

That is fundamentally different.

---

## Weakness

The Brief is still a format.

Not a visual identity.

A Brief is something Atlas produces.

Not necessarily the thing Atlas understands.

---

# Evaluation B — Atlas Insight

## Strengths

Simple.

Easy to scan.

Highly scalable.

Familiar.

Example:

```text
Atlas Insight

Bridge-focused firms currently
generate the highest interview rates.
```

---

## Weaknesses

Almost every modern SaaS product has:

```text
Insights
```

The term is overloaded.

It carries almost no identity.

Examples:

```text
Analytics Insights

Business Insights

Customer Insights

Marketing Insights
```

The word feels generic.

---

## Scalability

Excellent.

Identity value:

Low.

---

## Assessment

Insight should exist.

It should not become the defining Intelligence object.

---

# Alternative Exploration

The real question is:

What is the thing Atlas possesses?

Not:

What does Atlas display?

---

# Candidate Object

## Atlas Perspective

Definition:

```text
A Perspective is a coherent interpretation
of observed reality.
```

Examples:

```text
Transportation Perspective

Compensation Perspective

Career Perspective

Market Perspective

Skill Perspective
```

---

# Why Perspective Works

Signals answer:

```text
What exists?
```

Flows answer:

```text
What is happening?
```

Perspectives answer:

```text
What does it mean?
```

This is the missing layer.

---

# Example

```text
Atlas Perspective

Structural specialization is becoming
increasingly valuable.

Observation

Transportation firms generated
62% of interview activity.

Interpretation

Demand concentration appears strongest
among bridge-focused employers.

Implication

Generalist applications may underperform
specialized positioning.

Recommended Perspective

Prioritize transportation opportunities.
```

---

# Why Perspective Is Stronger Than Insight

An Insight is:

```text
Single observation.
```

A Perspective is:

```text
Interpretive framework.
```

That distinction matters.

Atlas should feel like it understands.

Not merely notices.

---

# Relationship To Lenses

This is where the Lens model becomes powerful.

Lens:

```text
How Atlas is looking.
```

Perspective:

```text
What Atlas sees through that lens.
```

Example:

```text
Career Lens
↓
Career Perspective
```

or

```text
Market Lens
↓
Market Perspective
```

This relationship feels natural.

---

# Relationship To Briefs

This resolves the Brief problem.

Perspective becomes:

```text
The object.
```

Brief becomes:

```text
The presentation format.
```

Equivalent to:

```text
Opportunity Signal
↓
Signal Card
```

and

```text
Atlas Perspective
↓
Atlas Brief
```

---

# Relationship To Recommendation Cards

Recommendation Card:

```text
Action
```

Perspective:

```text
Understanding
```

Recommendation Card answers:

```text
What should I do?
```

Perspective answers:

```text
Why does Atlas think that?
```

Both are necessary.

Neither replaces the other.

---

# Relationship To Ask Atlas

Perspective generates questions.

Ask Atlas resolves them.

Example:

```text
Perspective

Transportation firms currently provide
the strongest interview path.
```

User:

```text
Why?
```

Ask Atlas:

```text
Explanation
```

Flow:

```text
Perspective
↓
Question
↓
Investigation
```

---

# Final Recommendation

## Primary Intelligence Object

### Atlas Perspective

The Intelligence workspace should be built around Perspectives.

Not Insights.

Not Recommendations.

Not Reports.

Not Analytics.

---

## Supporting Object

### Atlas Brief

The Brief becomes the presentation format used to communicate a Perspective.

---

## Final Model

```text
Lens
↓
Perspective
↓
Brief
↓
Ask Atlas
```

This is the first model that fully aligns with:

```text
ATLAS scans.

Atlas interprets.

Ask Atlas communicates.
```

because Perspectives represent interpretation itself.

Not data.

Not metrics.

Not reports.

Understanding.
