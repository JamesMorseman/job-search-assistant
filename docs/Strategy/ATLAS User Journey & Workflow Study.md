# ATLAS User Journey & Workflow Study

Based on the accepted Product Strategy Report and Brand Specification, Donut's conclusion is that the most important design question is not:

> "How do users use ATLAS?"

It is:

> "Why do users return to ATLAS?"

A Career Intelligence System succeeds or fails based on repeated engagement. The workflow must create ongoing situational awareness rather than occasional application activity. The brand specification reinforces this directly through the dashboard philosophy of "Operational Awareness" and the core promise of understanding what matters, what changed, and what to do next.

---

# Core User Journey

The behavioral model should be:

```text
ATLAS scans
        ↓
Atlas interprets
        ↓
User reviews
        ↓
User acts
        ↓
Outcome recorded
        ↓
Atlas learns
        ↓
ATLAS scans again
```

This becomes the primary engagement loop.

The user should not feel like they are opening a dashboard.

They should feel like they are checking a mission control system.

---

# Daily Workflow

## User Goal

Answer:

> What requires my attention today?

This aligns directly with the brand specification's dashboard philosophy.

---

## Daily Journey

```text
Open ATLAS
      ↓
Review Changes
      ↓
Review Signals
      ↓
Review Recommendations
      ↓
Take Actions
      ↓
Leave
```

Target duration:

5–10 minutes

---

## Daily Review Questions

ATLAS should answer:

* What changed?
* What requires action?
* What opportunities appeared?
* What opportunities became stronger?
* What opportunities became weaker?
* What recommendations exist?

The user should never need to hunt for information.

---

## Daily Actions

Examples:

* Apply
* Follow up
* Schedule interview
* Research company
* Update materials
* Ignore recommendation

Most days should require very little effort.

# Active Job Search Workflow

This is the current primary ATLAS user.

---

## Workflow Diagram

```text
ATLAS Scans
      ↓
Opportunities Found
      ↓
Atlas Scores
      ↓
User Reviews
      ↓
Apply
      ↓
Track
      ↓
Follow Up
      ↓
Interview
      ↓
Offer
```

---

## User Objective

Reduce uncertainty.

The user wants confidence that:

* they are finding opportunities
* they are not missing opportunities
* they are applying efficiently
* they are improving results

---

## Atlas Role

Atlas should actively participate.

Examples:

* Better opportunity identified
* Application quality issue detected
* Follow-up recommended
* Response trend detected

High-value recommendations should be proactive.

# Passive Career Monitoring Workflow

This becomes the most important future workflow.

It creates retention.

It creates long-term value.

It creates commercial viability.

---

## Workflow Diagram

```text
ATLAS Monitors
       ↓
Market Changes
       ↓
Atlas Evaluates
       ↓
Significant Signal?
       ↓
No --------→ Silent
       ↓
Yes
       ↓
Notify User
```

---

## User Goal

Remain informed without active searching.

The user is employed.

The user is not applying.

The user still wants awareness.

---

## Signals Worth Surfacing

* Compensation changes
* New employers
* Promotion opportunities
* Skill demand changes
* Relocation opportunities
* Industry shifts

---

## Signals Not Worth Surfacing

* Minor fluctuations
* Low-confidence observations
* Recommendation noise

Silence is a feature.

---

# Atlas Workflow

Atlas exists to interpret.

Not to overwhelm.

This follows the product hierarchy:

ATLAS = platform

Atlas = intelligence layer

Ask Atlas = conversation layer.

---

# Recommendation Lifecycle

```text
Detection
      ↓
Evaluation
      ↓
Confidence Score
      ↓
Importance Score
      ↓
Recommendation
```

---

## When Atlas Should Speak

### High Confidence

Atlas is confident.

### High Impact

Meaningful outcome possible.

### Actionable

User can do something.

---

Examples:

* Better opportunity identified
* Interview probability improving
* Compensation trend emerging
* Skill gap discovered

---

## When Atlas Should Remain Silent

Low confidence.

Low impact.

No action available.

Recommendation fatigue destroys trust.

# Ask Atlas Workflow

Ask Atlas should not be the primary workflow.

This is a common mistake in AI products.

---

```text

      ↓

      ↓
```

Conversation supports work.

Conversation is not the work.

# Future End-State

The strongest long-term workflow is:

```text
ATLAS scans
       ↓
Atlas identifies meaningful change
       ↓
User reviews signal
       ↓
Ask Atlas explores implications
       ↓
User decides
       ↓
ATLAS monitors outcomes
```

This is the first workflow in the project where all three layers operate together exactly as intended:

**ATLAS scans.**

**Atlas interprets.**

**Ask Atlas communicates.**

That workflow is consistent with the Product Strategy Report's conclusion that ATLAS should evolve from Application Intelligence into Career Intelligence and ultimately Professional Intelligence, while remaining aligned with the Brand Specification's concept of continuous professional situational awareness.
